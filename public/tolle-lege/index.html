Turning readings into podcasts

One tap copies the source links and opens NotebookLM: make a notebook, choose Website, and paste. When you switch back to this page, the host instructions are copied for you; paste them under Audio Overview → Customize and generate. "Podcast the reading" appears on works whose full text is on New Advent (Church Fathers and Summa), and sends the text plus the thinker's 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia article. "Podcast the author" appears where New Advent has no text of the work, only the 1913 encyclopedia article on its author; the episode introduces the thinker, not the work, so it is preparation for reading rather than a substitute. Pick a course to get a course primer built from New Advent topic articles. You can download the finished episode in the NotebookLM app for offline listening. For other texts, download a PDF from the Internet Archive or Gutenberg link and upload it to NotebookLM instead.

Google offers no NotebookLM connection for personal accounts that a web page can call directly, so the last few taps happen inside NotebookLM.

About these links

Green links point to free online texts. New Advent hosts the 19th-century Ante-Nicene and Nicene/Post-Nicene Fathers translations and the 1920 English Dominican translation of the Summa theologiae. Other green links open a Project Gutenberg or Internet Archive search, because a single edition link can break. Look for a pre-1929 English edition, or the Latin, Greek or other original. For some medieval and modern works, only the original-language text is out of copyright. Catholic Encyclopedia links go to the 1913 edition on New Advent: it is public domain and good for background, but it is a century old and written from a Catholic standpoint. Gold-bordered links search WorldCat for library copies of works still under copyright.
